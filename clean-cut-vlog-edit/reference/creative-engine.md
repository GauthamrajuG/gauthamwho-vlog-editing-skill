# The creative engine: ideas nobody else would have

GauthamWho's bar: not "a cute graphic on a line". It should be a moment people remember and send
to a friend. The proof: the hooded ghost rising behind him on a dark stair walk-in. It had
nothing to do with the script. It was built for the person scrolling Reels alone at midnight,
and that viewer remembers who did it to them. This file is how to find moments like that on
purpose, in every clip.

Use it at the start of Job B, and whenever the creator asks "give me ideas" for a video.

## 1. Read the clip like a set designer (the opportunity inventory)

Before any idea, list what the footage actually offers, with timestamps:

- **Places:** dark corners, doorways, stairs, corridors, mirrors and reflections, windows at
  night, empty chairs, ceilings, the space behind his shoulders, under tables.
- **Light:** where it's dark (things can hide), where one lamp lights a wall (something can
  cast a shadow), flicker, his phone screen on his face.
- **Objects he touches or names:** the air fryer, the fridge, the scooty, food, the bag, a
  switch. Anything can come alive.
- **His words:** every figure of speech ("the world gets slow", "I'm dying of hunger", "this
  scooty is a rocket") and every claim.
- **His body:** pauses, glances off-camera, a wink, a turn, walking away. A glance can be
  "answered".
- **Time and context:** when it was shot, when people will watch (night uploads reach people
  in bed), the season and festivals, Tamil and Chennai references his audience shares.
- **Empty seconds:** b-roll and walk shots where nothing is said. These are free real estate.

## 2. Ten lenses (generate at least one idea per lens, never fewer than 10 raw ideas)

1. **Take the words literally.** The figure of speech actually happens: "the world gets slow"
   becomes a globe that slows down. Look for idioms, exaggerations and comparisons.
2. **The hidden passenger.** Something only the viewer sees. He never reacts (dramatic irony):
   the ghost. It must be easy to miss on first watch and impossible to unsee on the second.
3. **The audience's moment.** Design for when and where it's watched: midnight scare, lunch-hour
   hunger tease, the exam-season student. Ask "who is holding the phone, and where?"
4. **Objects with a personality.** A thing reacts to him: the air fryer gets impatient, the fridge
   exhales cold breath, the scooty's fuel needle sighs, the plate shivers when he picks it up.
5. **Physics breaks.** Gravity, scale or time misbehave for a second: crumbs float, a door is
   suddenly huge, steam forms a shape. Never a freeze-frame (rejected).
6. **Second-watch easter egg.** A detail that rewards a rewatch (rewatches add watch time): a face
   in the steam, a shadow with the wrong shape, a tiny figure on the stairs.
7. **Running lore.** Something that returns across reels: the ghost half-hidden in the next reel,
   the wrist-hand that clicks something in every video. Fans start hunting for it.
8. **The answered glance.** When he looks off-camera, something is there; when he pauses,
   something fills the pause.
9. **Scale shift.** A tiny world on his plate, a giant hand at the window, the scooty seen as a
   toy from above.
10. **Inversion.** Do the opposite of what the moment expects. A scary place gets something
    adorable; a cosy moment gets one wrong detail.

For more raw material when the list feels obvious, use provocation methods: an Oblique Strategy
card ("emphasise the flaws", "what would your closest friend do?"), SCAMPER (substitute, combine,
adapt, modify, put to another use, eliminate, reverse), and defamiliarisation (describe the
scene as if you'd never seen a kitchen).

## 3. The anti-generic filter (an idea must pass all of these)

- **The 10-second test.** If three other creators would think of it within 10 seconds of seeing
  the clip, drop it. The first idea is usually the generic one: generate past it.
- **Surprising but safe** (benign violation: McGraw & Warren 2010). It breaks a norm (physics,
  expectation, safety) and still feels okay. That comes from distance (it's a reel), a harmless
  reading (he's fine, it's a ghost "joke") or a weak norm. Too safe is boring; too real is
  upsetting. Never real harm, mockery of people, or anything cruel.
- **Fits the real frame.** There's space, light and depth for it, and something can hide it.
  An idea that needs a different shot is dropped, or offered as a reshoot note.
- **Doable here.** Remotion + three.js + SAM on this PC. No AI video, no paid services.
- **Serves him.** It never fights his message or steals his punchline. The face stays clear on
  his key lines.
- **Send-worthy.** Would someone send it to a friend with "did you see 0:36?" Instagram's top
  signals are watch time, likes per reach and **sends per reach** (Mosseri, Jan 2025).
- **Budget.** At most 1–3 graphic moments per reel, and only one "big" one. The rest is his video.

Score the survivors 1–5 on: surprise, memorability/sends, fit to the frame, effort (5 = easy),
and brand fit. Present the top 3 with a sketch each, and list the rest in one line each.

## 4. If it's a scare: how real jump scares are built

- **Build, then release.** Suspense (he walks into the dark, quiet) is separate from the scare
  that releases it.
- **Designed silence.** Lower the sound bed or let a natural quiet sit for 0.3–0.75 s before the
  peak.
- **A false scare first** (optional): a small harmless tease, like a sound or a shadow, lowers the
  guard. The real one comes slightly off the expected timing.
- **Sound and image on the same frame** at the peak. A soft but low hit, no shrieks (no harsh SFX).
- **Empathy.** We fear for him because he doesn't know it's there. Keep him unaware.
- **Leave fast.** The exit is quicker than the entrance. The viewer gets no time to study it, and
  rewatches instead.

## 5. If it's a visual joke

- The setup is his real moment, the twist is the graphic, and the reaction is his (real or
  implied by the cut).
- Funniest when it's deadpan: the impossible thing happens and life goes on.
- One joke per moment. Don't explain it.

## 6. Lore file

Keep `D:/Claude/Video/lore.md` (create it the first time): every approved recurring element,
with reel, time, what it did, how hidden it was, and the audience reaction if the creator
reports one. Check it at every new reel for a callback chance, and never repeat the same gag
the same way twice.

## 7. What to send the creator

One message with these parts:
- the opportunity inventory in 5–8 lines, with times;
- the top 3 ideas: time, what happens, why people will remember or send it, which lens it came
  from, the effort, and a one-line risk;
- the other 4–7 ideas in one line each;
- the question "Which one(s)? Or mix them."

Then storyboard → still → preview → full video, as in SKILL.md.
